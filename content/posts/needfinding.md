What techniques did you employ to discover users' needs?

The team conducted informal interviews with Grinnell students who had already gone through the process of planning their four year course schedule. Conversations focused on how students currently build and maintain their plans, what tools they rely on, and where the process feels most uncertain or stressful. We asked about how often plans change, how students track whether they are on track for their major, and what information they wish they had earlier. One interview was conducted with a third year student double majoring in Physics and Chemistry, whose responses gave us a detailed picture of the real planning experience.

What did you learn about users' needs from this process?

The interview revealed that four year planning at Grinnell is less of a fixed roadmap and more of a constantly shifting outline that students revise nearly every semester. The student we spoke with had changed their plan every semester since making it, primarily because of not getting into certain classes and having to find alternatives on short notice. A major source of frustration was not knowing in advance how likely they were to actually get into a planned course, or when class schedules would be released so they could spot conflicts early. The student also noted that their plan functioned more as a proof of concept that completing the major was feasible, rather than something they expected to follow precisely. What they valued most was being able to quickly verify they were still on track, which they currently did by manually comparing their transcript against the major requirements in Colleague Self-Service. These insights pointed clearly toward the need for a tool that could show requirement progress at a glance and make it easy to try out different course arrangements without having to redo the whole plan from scratch each time.
