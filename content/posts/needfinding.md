What techniques did you employ to discover users' needs?

The team conducted informal interviews with Grinnell students — specifically students who had recently gone through the process of planning their four-year course schedule. Conversations focused on pain points with the existing tools (the registrar's worksheet and the catalog PDF), how students currently track prerequisites, and where they felt uncertain about whether they were on track for their major. We also observed how students currently lay out their plans using spreadsheets or handwritten grids.

What did you learn about users' needs from this process?

Students consistently wanted two things the existing tools did not provide: (1) automatic prerequisite checking so they would not accidentally schedule a course before its prerequisite, and (2) a live view of whether their current plan satisfied major requirements. We learned that students frequently discover prerequisite conflicts only when a registration hold blocks them at enrollment time, and that requirement tracking was done through separate manual lookups in the catalog. These two gaps directly shaped the core features of the planner.
