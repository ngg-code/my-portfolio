Needfinding

Answers (Mihail (Misho) Palmov): 1. 3rd year 2. Physics and Chemistry 3. Before starting school at Grinnell I had no information on course planning and did not know how I would structure my degree. 4. I looked at the major requirements, when I was declaring my majors, and did my best to fit all the classes I needed in a way that would not make my schedule too hectic. 5. I used the major declaration form and separate sheets of paper to write different versions of my 4-year plan. 6. Before talking to my advisors, my 4-year plan was very tentative and was only based on the few classes I knew I had to take. In my initial 4-year plan, I did not think about courses, outside of my majors very much. 7. Not knowing if I will be able to get into certain classes, that I either need for my major or want to take. As an example, in my four-year plan I was going to take two STEM and two humanities classes this semester, however I was cut from both of my humanities and could only find one other non-STEM course that matched my schedule. The fact that my four-year plan is subject to change every semester and has to be flexible to accommodate that is very frustrating. 8. No, typically prerequisites are well explained, and so are major requirements. I have, however, had trouble with repeatedly having to get prerequisites waived, as I came in with outside credit. While this didn't affect my four-year plan it makes course registration slightly more stressful. 9. I have had to change my four-year plan every semester since I first made it. 10. I have changed my four-year plan once because the classes I was taking make the workload too much (planning error on my part), and because I did not get into some of my classes another time. 11. It is fairly easy to tell that I'm on track both thanks to my advisors, who are always clear on my progress, and thanks to Colleague Self-Service, which nicely presents my progress . 12. I go back to my academic transcript or Colleague Self-Service and compare it with the major requirements. 13. Having a good, flexible plan for my four years is very calming and generally helpful. The problem for me is that this plan is more-so a theoretical proof-of-concept that I can complete my major, rather than a plan that is set in stone. 14. I think it would be helpful to stress more on your four year plan early on in your First Year, as it gives a good template to follow in our next three years. 17.
